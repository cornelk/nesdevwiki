The **Ricoh RP2A03** is the main processor used in [Nintendo Entertainment System (NES)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nintendo_Entertainment_System) and its region variants. It consists of a [MOS Technology 6502](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MOS_Technology_6502) microprocessor core (lacking decimal mode), [5-channel programmable sound generator](../APU/), joypad I/O, and [DMA](../DMA/) devices. PAL systems contains RP2A07, which has minor differences compared to the NTSC version, such as a different clock divider, adjusted sampled audio rates, and [DMA](../DMA/) bugfixes.

## 2A03 register map {#2A03_register_map}

In addition to the registers in the 6502 core, the 2A03 provides its own 22 memory-mapped registers for sound generation (see [NES APU](../APU/)), joystick I/O, and [OAM DMA](../PPU_registers/#OAMDMA) transferring. Unlike the addresses of [PPU](../PPU/) registers and [mapper](../Mapper/) registers, CPU register addresses are completely decoded, which means that the entire space from the end of CPU registers to the top of address space (\$4020 through \$FFFF) is available to the Game Pak.

The range \$4018-\$401F does nothing on a retail NES. It was intended for 2A03 functionality that never made it to production. Various revisions of the 2A03 include [test registers](../CPU_Test_Mode/) (which are disabled in normal operation) or [remnants of an incompletely implemented IRQ counter](../RP2A03_Programmable_Interval_Timer/) that was disconnected from the rest of the circuit. Mappers can place writable registers here without conflicting with the 2A03, but placing readable registers here should be avoided because of [conflicts with DMA](../DMA/#Register_conflicts).
